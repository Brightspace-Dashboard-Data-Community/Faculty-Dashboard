Microsoft PowerPoint

Slides are read in a specific order by assistive technology; titles, layouts, and reading order all matter.

Slides & layouts

  • Start from accessible built-in themes and slide layouts with title placeholders.
  • Every slide should have a unique, descriptive title (even if you hide the title visually on a layout that still keeps it for assistive tech—prefer layouts that show the title when possible).
  • Avoid critical information only in speaker notes if students rely on the file without narration.

Reading order

Screen readers follow the internal order of objects on a slide, which may differ from left-to-right visual order.

  • Open Home → Arrange → Selection Pane (Windows) or the equivalent on your platform.
  • List order is often read bottom to top; drag items so the sequence matches the intended reading flow.
  • Group related objects when it makes sense, and remove off-slide or decorative duplicates from the order.

Images & media

  • Add alt text to images and icons that carry meaning.
  • Embed or link to captioned video; describe important motion if audio alone is not enough.

Verify

  • Use Review → Check Accessibility before exporting or uploading to D2L.
